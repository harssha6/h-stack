# Original Thinking

Help the user make something specific to its purpose. Produce a point of view,
not novelty for its own sake. Apply this skill when the choice shapes what the
product is, how it behaves, or why someone would remember it. A login page can
qualify; a routine CSS fix usually does not.

## Divide The Decisions

- **Prove:** contracts, existing behavior, data ownership, accessibility,
  performance, security, and whether the real flow works. Inspect and test
  these without asking the user to decide facts for you.
- **Recommend:** product structure, technical tradeoffs, interaction models,
  visual language, copy posture, and where to depart from convention. Form a
  reasoned view and explain its cost.
- **Invite judgment:** choices between defensible directions that express
  different identities or change the user's intended audience, taste, or
  ambition. Show concrete options and recommend one. Do not make the user
  arbitrate details the agent can settle from evidence.

These categories can overlap. An architecture decision may have measurable
constraints and a subjective product tradeoff. Name both.

## When Shaping A Direction

1. Identify the user, the moment, the job, and what the current product or
   reference material already establishes. Seek the real surface when one
   exists.
2. State a product thesis in plain language: what should this experience make
   easier, clearer, or more distinctive, and why?
3. Generate at least one direction from that thesis before borrowing a visual
   reference or default pattern. Say what the reference contributes and where
   this product should differ.
4. Challenge a merely plausible answer. Ask whether the same result could be
   transplanted into an unrelated product unchanged. If so, find the missing
   product-specific decision.
5. Present a small number of genuinely different options only when the
   unresolved choice warrants the user's taste. Include a recommendation,
   tradeoff, and a testable artifact when practical.
6. Once the direction is chosen or already clear, execute it. Preserve precise
   user corrections; do not reopen settled taste decisions.

## When Editing A Built Product

Experience the complete relevant flow as a user. Compare it with the thesis and
inspect transitions between screens, states, copy, data, and failure recovery.
Identify the few decisions that weaken usefulness, coherence, or character.
Protect details that make the product fit its audience. Prioritize changes by
their effect on the experience, then verify the changed flow in the real surface.
Passing tests and visual polish alone do not establish that the product is good.

## Boundaries

- Do not delay a narrow requested fix for a concept exercise.
- Do not invent a brand position from thin evidence. Mark a proposed direction
  as a proposal and keep reversible exploration moving.
- Do not manufacture disagreement or ask for approval on every creative detail.
  Exploring and testing candidates does not approve a lasting product direction
  or promote a candidate into a reusable library; ask the user at that boundary.
- Do not force novelty into safety, accessibility, familiar controls, or
  operational tools where predictability serves the user.
- Keep project-specific intent in the project's current sources. This skill
  holds the method, not a snapshot of any product.
